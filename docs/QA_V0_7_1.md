# v0.7.1 — interface corrections

2026-11-01. Scope: the user's four interface corrections to v0.7.0.

- Numeric scene sensors retain their physical projected anchors instead of
  detached corner rows. Reserved dimensions and tabular digits prevent value-driven
  resizing/repositioning; camera or component movement legitimately moves a callout.
- The MGU-K controls have consistent group gaps, aligned label/value columns,
  a model-selector margin and no disabled pedal sliders in the guided view.
- Rendering has a fixed 200 FPS ceiling, with no selector and no stored cap.
  The optional statistics counter remains. Physics still advances at 1/240 s.
- Manual pedals are momentary buttons with gradual response, mouse capture,
  touch cancellation, Space/Enter and existing W/S/arrows. Releasing outside the
  button or losing focus cannot leave a held pedal. Brake priority is retained.

No source in `src/physics/` changed. The guided scenario still reaches 100 km/h
and releases 2.4691358 MJ for 800 kg, with the existing energy-ledger checks.

## Verification

- All 552 unit tests pass, including 200 FPS scheduling above the cap, unchanged
  fixed-step time and numeric callouts following their physical anchor.
- Lint, typecheck, formatting and production build pass.
- Initial targeted browser run: 31 pass, one Lenz status-badge overlap.
  Refreshing dynamic overlay obstacles before every placement fixed that stale
  geometry; both Lenz resolutions passed on the follow-up.
- Follow-up: all four cases pass, including real mouse/keyboard/touch cancellation,
  unchanged callout bounding boxes across signed/different-length numbers, and
  callouts moving with the camera instead of remaining detached at screen corners.
- The initial targeted run also passed all eight five-second fixed-panel/sensor-size
  recordings, fullscreen checks, guided braking energy and the new pedal controls.
- The complete browser suite remains the CI gate before merge/tag. Scene overlap
  thresholds and regression coverage are unchanged. The optional performance
  sampler now records the fixed 200 FPS policy, without the removed selector.

The 200 FPS setting is a ceiling, not a claim that the monitor/GPU reaches 200 FPS.
No new hardware or native-runtime performance claim is made.
